# ZAISAKU — THE ART & DESIGN CONSTITUTION

## 01. Permanent instruction

Save this document as `docs/ZAISAKU-ART-CONSTITUTION.md`.

Reference it prominently from `AGENTS.md` and all relevant art direction, asset production, world architecture and interface documentation.

Every future development agent working on ZAISAKU must read this document before making visual, environmental, interface or artistic decisions.

This document establishes the permanent creative foundation of ZAISAKU.

Where older art direction documents conflict with this constitution, this constitution takes precedence, while preserving explicitly approved artwork and creator decisions.

Do not gradually dilute this identity through unrelated asset generation, generic design conventions or arbitrary stylistic changes.

The visual identity must become stronger as the game expands.

---

# PART I — THE SOUL OF ZAISAKU

## 02. What ZAISAKU is

ZAISAKU is a living economic civilisation set on Zuisaku, an entirely different planet.

It is not Earth in the future.

It is not an alternative version of Japan.

It is not a medieval kingdom.

It is not a post-apocalyptic wasteland.

Zuisaku has its own evolutionary history, materials, biology, chemistry, technology, culture, spirituality and relationship with physical objects.

Its inhabitants have developed ways of living, manufacturing, trading and understanding reality that occasionally resemble our own, but are fundamentally different.

The player should recognise enough to feel comfortable, while encountering enough unfamiliarity to remain curious.

The world should feel as though it existed long before the player arrived and will continue existing after they leave.

## 03. The emotional identity

ZAISAKU should simultaneously feel:

- Beautiful.
- Quiet.
- Cool.
- Curious.
- Tactile.
- Sophisticated.
- Mysterious.
- Intimate.
- Slightly unsettling.
- Melancholic.
- Occasionally comforting.
- Intellectually intriguing.
- Alive.

It should possess understated confidence.

Never desperate to impress. Never excessively cute. Never excessively serious.

There should be something effortlessly stylish about it.

A player should occasionally stop moving simply because a particular corner of the world feels good to inhabit.

## 04. The meaning of dopeness

ZAISAKU must have taste.

Its coolness should emerge from unexpected combinations, precise composition, unusual objects, confident restraint and original ideas.

Think about the artistic confidence of an underground record sleeve, an obscure electronic instrument, an independent design studio or a forgotten technological artefact.

Not luxury for the sake of luxury.

Not futuristic decoration for the sake of looking futuristic.

Not excessive detail for the sake of demonstrating technical ability.

Make things that people would genuinely want to look at, collect, screenshot, own and remember.

An ordinary object can be incredibly cool when its shape, material, purpose and history are considered carefully.

ZAISAKU should feel designed by people with excellent taste, not assembled by a generator that knows the words pixel art and steampunk.

## 05. Originality over references

External references can help explain individual qualities, but no existing game should become the visual template for ZAISAKU.

Avoid directly reproducing the aesthetics of Stardew Valley, Eastward, Pokémon, RuneScape, Hyper Light Drifter or conventional pixel-art RPGs.

Likewise, Teenage Engineering, Braun and premium electronic instruments may influence interface restraint and industrial design thinking, but must not become direct visual copies.

ZAISAKU should eventually become its own reference.

Someone should be able to see an unlabelled screenshot, object or interface and recognise where it belongs.

# PART II — MINERAL ANALOG

## 06. The world art style

The permanent environmental art direction is called **Mineral Analog**.

Mineral Analog combines:

- Modern pixel art.
- Organic industrial design.
- Unfamiliar mineral architecture.
- Analog technology.
- Living materials.
- Restrained biopunk.
- Quiet science fiction.
- Environmental storytelling.
- Geometric visual language.

It is not steampunk.

It is not traditional fantasy.

It is not cyberpunk.

It is not conventional retro-futurism.

It is a visual culture developed around materials and natural phenomena that belong specifically to Zuisaku.

## 07. A modern approach to pixel art

Pixel art is an artistic medium, not an obligation to reproduce the limitations of old games.

ZAISAKU should use pixel art with modern composition, contemporary design sensibility and sophisticated visual systems.

Combine:

- Crisp, deliberate pixel clusters.
- Strong silhouettes.
- Selective material texture.
- Meaningful shading.
- Modern lighting.
- Smooth camera movement.
- Dynamic environmental effects.
- High-quality typography.
- Responsive interfaces.
- Procedural atmospheric animation.

Do not artificially make everything look like a game from 1995.

Do not force every interface, font, icon or animation into pixel-art constraints.

The contrast between illustrated pixel-art environments and exceptionally clean modern interfaces is intentional.

Use the strengths of each medium.

## 08. Pixel density and detail

ZAISAKU's artwork should sit between extremely simple pixel sprites and excessively elaborate illustrated pixel art.

The ideal sprite is:

- Readable at gameplay scale.
- Recognisable by silhouette.
- Rich enough to possess character.
- Simple enough to reproduce consistently.
- Detailed only where detail adds meaning.

Avoid both extremes:

- Primitive rectangles pretending to be finished artwork.
- Highly detailed AI illustrations pretending to be usable game sprites.

Graphical complexity should not become the primary source of beauty.

Use a limited number of carefully selected details.

## 09. Perspective

The world uses fixed angled top-down orthographic 2D perspective.

The player moves on a square logical grid, with smooth movement between positions.

The art should show sufficient upper surfaces and vertical faces to communicate physical depth.

Do not mix:

- Side-view assets.
- Fully overhead assets.
- True isometric assets.
- Arbitrary three-quarter illustrations.
- Incorrectly rotated directional sprites.

Every location must follow the same spatial logic.

Directional architecture must be drawn for its actual orientation.

A south-facing building cannot become an east-facing building simply by rotating its PNG.

## 10. Depth is essential

ZAISAKU must never feel like a flat board with stickers placed on it.

Depth should emerge from:

- Coherent perspective.
- Visible roof surfaces.
- Architectural thickness.
- Foundations.
- Ground contact.
- Layered vegetation.
- Correct occlusion.
- Meaningful shadows.
- Different object heights.
- Carefully composed foreground elements.
- Natural spatial relationships.

Buildings should feel constructed.

Furniture should feel placed.

Characters should feel physically present.

Collectibles should feel like objects with volume and weight.

A front and a roof must belong to one believable structure, not appear as disconnected pictures.

## 11. Scale and physical proportions

Maintain a coherent relationship between characters, architecture, furniture and objects.

A person should plausibly fit through a doorway.

A chair should look usable.

A counter should have a believable working height.

A building should feel substantially larger than its inhabitants.

A tree should occupy meaningful physical space.

Do not make objects the same visual size simply because their source PNGs have the same dimensions.

The logical grid is not an artistic restriction.

Maintain separate definitions for:

- Source resolution.
- Display resolution.
- Physical footprint.
- Visual bounds.
- Collision geometry.
- Interaction position.
- Ground anchor.

# PART III — MATERIALS, COLOUR AND ARCHITECTURE

## 12. The material identity

ZAISAKU's visual language is based on a small family of recurring materials.

These materials should appear throughout architecture, technology, furniture, infrastructure and collectibles.

### Vey

Living ceramic.

Warm bone, ivory and mineral cream.

Smooth, dense, tactile and occasionally organic.

Vey is used in architectural shells, domestic objects, instruments and manufacturing.

Its surfaces should feel crafted rather than plastic.

### Oru

Green-grey mineral alloy.

Muted olive, charcoal-green and mineral grey.

Heavy, durable and structurally important.

Oru is used in machinery, frames, infrastructure and industrial objects.

### Sera

Resonant matter.

Amber, ochre and controlled warm illumination.

Used in unusual instruments, energy systems and scientific equipment.

Its illumination should suggest meaningful activity rather than decorative LEDs everywhere.

### Supporting materials

Additional materials may include:

- Dark mineral composites.
- Muted brass.
- Mineral glass.
- Woven biological fibres.
- Oxidised metals.
- Burgundy textile or lacquer.
- Subdued native organic matter.

These materials should feel physically distinct.

Do not introduce dozens of unrelated textures without a reason.

## 13. Colour philosophy

The global visual palette is mineral, warm, muted and confident.

A starting colour vocabulary includes:

- Charcoal carbon.
- Bone ceramic.
- Green-grey mineral.
- Deep olive.
- Muted brass.
- Warm amber.
- Oxide burgundy.
- Subdued atmospheric teal.

Use stronger colours selectively.

Amber signifies activity, illumination, resonance or important state.

Burgundy can communicate identity, ceremony, consequence or visual emphasis.

Bone provides architectural structure and interface contrast.

Olive provides continuity across technology and the environment.

Charcoal provides visual rest.

Avoid turning the entire planet into a monochromatic brown-and-olive world.

Different regions may introduce their own subdued colour families while retaining the established materials and design principles.

Regional variation is encouraged. Stylistic inconsistency is not.

## 14. Architectural identity

ZAISAKU's buildings should resemble a civilisation that designs architecture using the same principles it uses to manufacture instruments.

Recurring shapes include:

- Rounded rectangular shells.
- Stepped mineral structures.
- Circular observation windows.
- Recessed doorways.
- Integrated light sources.
- Thick ceramic edges.
- Modular structural courses.
- Embedded conduits.
- Asymmetrical functional details.
- Distinctive roof silhouettes.

Think about enlarged scientific instruments, cultivated structures and unfamiliar domestic appliances.

Avoid relying on conventional pitched roofs, medieval towers, wooden fantasy houses, generic brick buildings or stereotypical Japanese architecture.

Architecture should be purposeful.

An exchange, research facility, residence and duel hall should feel different, while clearly belonging to the same civilisation.

## 15. Architecture must have depth and purpose

Buildings must not exist purely as decorative facades.

Where practical, architectural identity should communicate:

- What happens inside.
- Who uses the building.
- Which materials it is constructed from.
- Whether it is domestic, industrial, commercial or ceremonial.
- How inhabitants approach and enter it.

Use visual clues rather than permanent text labels.

A research building may have an integrated observation lens.

A duel building may echo the shape of its exchange instrument.

A residence may have softer materials, warmer windows and a lower silhouette.

Do not make every building a variation of the same decorated box.

# PART IV — BIOLOGY, CULTURE AND OTHERWORLDLINESS

## 16. Less Japanese, more Zuisaku

ZAISAKU can retain subtle influences from Japanese design philosophy:

- Restraint.
- Craftsmanship.
- Negative space.
- Precision.
- Appreciation of objects.
- Quiet attention to detail.

However, overt Japanese visual motifs should not dominate.

Avoid repetitive use of:

- Kanji signage.
- Torii gates.
- Traditional Japanese roofs.
- Cherry blossom trees.
- Samurai imagery.
- Anime conventions.
- Japanese clothing stereotypes.
- Conventional East Asian temple architecture.

The name ZAISAKU does not imply that Zuisaku is Japan.

Develop a native culture with its own visual logic.

## 17. Native biology

Zuisaku's biology should be unfamiliar without becoming excessively alien.

Established biological families include:

- Disc trees.
- Lantern growth.
- Mineral reeds.
- Veinflora.

These families should develop recognisable shapes, materials, growth patterns and environmental behaviours.

Native organisms may interact with mineral matter and resonance in ways that differ from Earth.

Their designs should be simple enough to reproduce consistently.

Avoid creating an entirely unrelated species every time a plant is needed.

Establish biological families that can evolve through:

- Growth stages.
- Regional variants.
- Environmental conditions.
- Cultivation.
- Material composition.
- Subtle mutations.

The world should communicate that biology and technology developed together.

## 18. The inhabitants

Characters should have distinctive, understated silhouettes.

Preserve approved character identities.

Avoid excessively cute chibi characters, generic anime heroes or conventional fantasy classes.

Inhabitants may wear:

- Mineral sensory devices.
- Visors.
- Work coats.
- Unfamiliar accessories.
- Protective clothing.
- Cultural objects.
- Resonance-related instruments.

Their appearance should communicate occupation, personality and environment.

Their precise biological nature need not always be obvious.

Maintain a balance between familiarity and ambiguity.

Use consistent character proportions and a shared animation architecture.

## 19. Cultural design language

Develop a small, consistent library of native symbols.

These symbols can represent:

- Exchange.
- Research.
- Storage.
- Resonance.
- Transit.
- Residence.
- Cultivation.
- Manufacturing.
- Auctions.
- Contracts.

Use simple geometric forms with a recognisable internal logic.

Symbols should appear consistently on architecture, instruments, packaging and interfaces.

Do not invent a new arbitrary glyph for every object.

Avoid meaningless pseudo-Japanese writing.

Use actual dynamic text wherever information needs to be understood.

The fictional visual language should enrich the world, not obstruct usability.

## 20. The ordinary and the extraordinary

The extraordinary only feels extraordinary when it exists alongside the ordinary.

Use this approximate balance as a creative guideline:

- 70% understandable everyday civilisation.
- 25% unfamiliar technology, biology and materials.
- 5% genuinely mysterious phenomena.

Do not fill every room with magical artefacts, glowing crystals and unexplained machinery.

Allow the player to discover strangeness gradually.

An ordinary-looking object that behaves unexpectedly can be more memorable than an enormous glowing supernatural structure.

# PART V — ATMOSPHERE AND ENVIRONMENTAL COMPOSITION

## 21. The world must be quietly alive

ZAISAKU should feel alive even when the player is doing nothing.

Use subtle environmental movement:

- Lantern plants shifting slightly.
- Canopies moving gently.
- Occasional fabric movement.
- Slow changes in instrument indicators.
- Local light fluctuations.
- Small biological pulses.
- Rare atmospheric particles.
- restrained environmental effects.

These movements should be irregular, meaningful and understated.

Avoid making every sprite constantly animate.

Avoid identical movement patterns across different organisms.

Stillness is part of the atmosphere.

Movement should make the stillness more beautiful.

## 22. Shadows

Shadows are fundamental to the visual identity.

Every meaningful object should feel connected to its environment.

Use:

- Stable character contact shadows.
- Appropriate furniture shadows.
- Architectural grounding.
- Roof overhang shadows.
- Subtle tree shadows.
- Localised environmental shading.

Avoid enormous black circles beneath every object.

Avoid double shadows where source artwork already includes shading.

Maintain consistent lighting direction.

Shadows must never interfere with collision, navigation or hit testing.

## 23. Light

Illumination should feel physical, warm and intentional.

Warm windows, lamps, living organisms and instruments should gently illuminate nearby surfaces.

The source of a light should be understandable.

Use controlled local glows and limited bloom.

Do not make glowing outlines the default aesthetic.

Avoid turning the world excessively dark simply because warm lights look dramatic.

The art must remain readable on ordinary mobile screens.

Use contrast carefully.

## 24. Resonance

Resonance is ZAISAKU's signature phenomenon.

It should have a recognisable visual language that appears throughout the world, technology and economy.

Use:

- Geometric rings.
- Interference patterns.
- Small points of light.
- Oscillations.
- Structured pulses.
- Material-specific colours.
- Brief connections between compatible objects.
- Unfamiliar patterns that suggest natural laws.

Resonance is not generic fantasy magic.

It should feel like a physical property of Zuisaku.

Use procedural rendering and reusable animation systems wherever possible.

The strongest resonance effects should be meaningful and relatively uncommon.

## 25. Environmental storytelling

Use spatial composition to imply life, history and function.

A parcel beside a scale.

A research instrument beside an unfinished specimen.

A lamp beside a sleeping area.

An empty display stand waiting for something valuable.

A quiet commercial space after its owner has left.

Objects should appear where inhabitants would naturally place them.

Avoid randomly filling empty tiles with decorative props.

A small, carefully composed scene can communicate more than a large collection of unrelated assets.

## 26. Negative space

Negative space is a permanent part of ZAISAKU's visual language.

Not every area needs decoration.

Not every wall needs an instrument.

Not every corner needs a plant.

Not every interface needs a frame.

Create a visual rhythm between busy and quiet spaces.

Give important objects room to breathe.

The player should be able to appreciate the artwork rather than being overwhelmed by it.

## 27. Environmental variety

Different locations should have different moods.

A residence can be warm and intimate.

An exchange can be structured and purposeful.

A research facility can feel precise and quietly strange.

An industrial district can feel heavy and oxidised.

A cultivation district can feel biological and patient.

Unmapped territories can be sparse and unsettling.

Use changes in:

- Spatial layout.
- Regional palette.
- Light temperature.
- Material emphasis.
- Biological families.
- Ambient behaviour.
- Architecture.
- Environmental density.

Do not require an entirely new art style for each location.

## 28. The world should be worth inhabiting

A player should sometimes want to remain in a location even when there is no immediate economic reason.

The atmosphere should create attachment.

Returning home should feel different from entering a market.

Discovering a new district should feel like visiting another part of a real planet.

A rare object should feel like something that deserves a place on a shelf.

The world is not merely a navigation layer for accessing menus.

It is the emotional foundation of the game.

# PART VI — OBJECT DESIGN

## 29. Objects are the heart of ZAISAKU

Every collectible should feel like something that could exist within this civilisation.

Objects may be:

- Ordinary.
- Beautiful.
- Strange.
- Useful.
- Broken.
- Valuable.
- Forgotten.
- Misunderstood.
- Historically significant.
- Biologically active.
- Technologically sophisticated.

Avoid generic fantasy loot.

An object should not require an exaggerated rarity colour or elaborate particle effect to become interesting.

Its visual identity, behaviour, provenance and story should create desirability.

## 30. Industrial design for fictional objects

Design each object as if it were manufactured, cultivated or assembled by inhabitants of Zuisaku.

Consider:

- Purpose.
- Material.
- Construction.
- Age.
- Handling.
- Wear.
- Manufacturer.
- Unusual properties.
- Cultural significance.

The object should have a distinctive silhouette.

Prefer a small number of memorable design decisions over unnecessary detail.

Objects should share the native material language without all looking identical.

## 31. Consistent object identity

Every object definition has one canonical visual identity.

Its artwork must remain consistent across:

- Market.
- Vault.
- Auctions.
- Duels.
- Inspection.
- Residence.
- Physical display.
- Future player-owned businesses.

Never generate an unrelated illustration of the same object for a different screen.

Additional resolutions, perspectives and animations must preserve its identity.

Use stable asset IDs and shared artwork registries.

## 32. Desirability

The player should want to own an object partly because it looks interesting.

Some objects should be attractive even before their economic or gameplay properties are understood.

Make collections feel personal.

Use subtle visual differences, meaningful silhouettes and distinct materials.

Avoid making every rare item simply bigger, brighter or more decorated.

Taste is more important than spectacle.

# PART VII — QUIET INSTRUMENT INTERFACE

## 33. A separate but complementary visual medium

The universal ZAISAKU interface is called **Quiet Instrument**.

The world is illustrated pixel art.

The interface is modern industrial design.

This distinction is deliberate.

Quiet Instrument should feel like premium technology designed and manufactured on Zuisaku.

It should be refined, readable, tactile and quietly distinctive.

Use CSS, SVG, dynamic typography and reusable design tokens wherever practical.

Do not rasterise ordinary interface controls unnecessarily.

## 34. Interface identity

Prefer:

- Dark charcoal surfaces.
- Bone ceramic primary controls.
- Muted olive secondary surfaces.
- Restrained amber state indicators.
- Burgundy for meaningful consequences.
- Precise spacing.
- Consistent corner radii.
- Clear hierarchy.
- Compact symbols.
- Large interaction targets.
- Quiet micro-interactions.

Avoid:

- Medieval fantasy borders.
- Decorative gold frames.
- Excessive pixel fonts.
- Generic mobile-game gradients.
- Floating promotional badges.
- Oversized glows.
- Unnecessary labels.
- Casino presentation.
- Excessive shadows on every panel.

The interface should complement the artwork rather than compete with it.

## 35. Typography

Typography must remain dynamic, readable and interchangeable.

Never bake text, prices, numbers, descriptions, labels or translations into artwork.

Support:

- Different fonts.
- Unicode.
- Polish characters.
- Additional languages.
- Longer translated labels.
- Accessibility settings.
- Locale-aware formatting.

Use typography as a deliberate artistic element.

Balance distinctive character with legibility.

Do not sacrifice readability simply to maintain a retro appearance.

## 36. Interaction quality

Every interface action should feel immediate and deliberate.

Use subtle feedback through:

- Small state changes.
- Restrained colour transitions.
- Mechanical-feeling confirmations.
- Precise movement.
- Brief illumination.
- Quiet sound where appropriate.

Avoid exaggerated bouncing, unnecessary animation delays and excessive popups.

The player should never feel that the interface is wasting their time.

A premium interface is defined as much by responsiveness and clarity as by its appearance.

## 37. Mobile and desktop

Both platforms are equally important.

Mobile interfaces must be deliberately composed for touch.

Desktop interfaces must use additional space intelligently.

Do not simply shrink desktop layouts.

Do not artificially enlarge every world sprite on mobile.

Keep essential information accessible.

Maintain a coherent visual language across portrait mobile, landscape mobile and desktop.

# PART VIII — SEALED EXCHANGE

## 38. The duel identity

The duel is called **Sealed Exchange** within the visual design system.

It should feel like operating a physical competitive trading instrument from Zuisaku.

Use the approved Pack 05 artwork and compatible subsequent assets.

The instrument can be more detailed and tactile than ordinary menus.

Its design should combine:

- Mineral ceramic construction.
- Recessed object compartments.
- Mechanical bid controls.
- Sealed decision chambers.
- Amber indicators.
- Dynamic displays.
- Resonance behaviour.
- Restrained ceremonial qualities.

The duel must not look like an unrelated digital card game.

## 39. Competitive clarity

Artistic detail must never interfere with gameplay.

The player should immediately understand:

- Available objects.
- Current valuations.
- Balance.
- Inventory.
- Round.
- Countdown.
- Available decisions.
- Private objectives.
- Public opponent information.
- Resolution.

All live values remain dynamic.

Never reveal private opponent information through graphical effects.

Use variable object trays rather than decorative fixed compartments that break when inventory changes.

The instrument should feel physical while remaining exceptionally efficient to use.

## 40. Future duel themes

Future districts may develop their own versions of Sealed Exchange instruments.

They can differ through:

- Material.
- Shape.
- Illumination.
- Regional colour.
- Manufacturing style.
- Cultural details.

They must retain consistent competitive usability.

Cosmetic themes must not influence gameplay outcomes or confer informational advantages.

Do not create theme variations that undermine the global ZAISAKU identity.

# PART IX — MOVEMENT AND GAME FEEL

## 41. Intention-based navigation

The primary world interaction philosophy is:

**Tap what you want to do, not how you want to get there.**

The player taps a destination to walk.

Taps an inhabitant to approach and converse.

Taps an object to approach and interact.

Taps a doorway to enter.

The game handles navigation, pathfinding and approach positioning.

Do not reintroduce a mobile joystick.

Desktop keyboard movement may remain optional.

The experience should feel modern, intuitive and comfortable with one finger.

## 42. Movement as visual craftsmanship

Movement should feel physically grounded and responsive.

Maintain:

- Stable foot anchors.
- Coherent walk animations.
- Natural arrival positions.
- Responsive cancellation.
- Correct facing directions.
- Smooth camera following.
- Appropriate depth sorting.

Avoid sliding, snapping, animation flicker and unnecessary zigzags.

The player should never have to fight the navigation system.

## 43. Interaction feedback in the world

Use subtle visual language to acknowledge player intention.

A small ground marker.

A brief geometric response.

A restrained highlight.

A meaningful contextual state.

Avoid large arrows, permanent glowing outlines and excessive floating interface elements.

The world itself should communicate what can be interacted with.

# PART X — SOUND AND SENSORY IDENTITY

## 44. Sound should belong to Zuisaku

Where audio is present, it should follow the same artistic philosophy.

Potential characteristics:

- Warm analog textures.
- Soft mechanical clicks.
- Unusual resonant tones.
- Organic environmental sounds.
- Restrained synthetic ambience.
- Quiet rhythmic details.
- Subtle imperfections.

Avoid generic fantasy orchestration, stereotypical Japanese music and conventional cyberpunk sound design.

Do not fill every moment with audio.

Silence can be meaningful.

Music and ambience should be independently adjustable.

## 45. Music philosophy

ZAISAKU's music should feel like something that could only belong to this world.

Electronic, organic, atmospheric and occasionally rhythmically interesting.

It may incorporate the warmth of analog instruments, unusual harmonies, understated percussion and experimental textures.

Do not reduce the soundtrack to repetitive generic lo-fi background music.

The creator is a music producer. Leave room for an original musical identity rather than locking the game into stock music.

# PART XI — AI-EXPANDABLE ART PRODUCTION

## 46. Simplicity of production is a creative constraint

ZAISAKU must remain practical for a solo creator working with AI.

The art direction must be sufficiently distinctive without requiring a professional illustrator to manually create every new object.

Prefer:

- Reusable architectural modules.
- Shared material palettes.
- Repeatable proportions.
- Native biological families.
- Standard sprite anchors.
- Modular character components.
- Shared interface systems.
- Procedural lighting.
- Reusable visual effects.
- Data-driven location definitions.

Uniqueness should emerge from the combination of simple, consistent rules.

Do not create an art direction that becomes impossible to maintain after the first district.

## 47. Approved reference library

Maintain a canonical library of approved visual references.

Include:

- Architecture.
- Characters.
- Native vegetation.
- Materials.
- Terrain.
- Collectibles.
- Furniture.
- Exchange instruments.
- Interface components.
- Actual approved gameplay screenshots.

Future asset generation should reference approved examples, not rely entirely on text prompts.

Do not generate each pack from an empty creative context.

Preserve the visual DNA.

## 48. Asset specifications

Respect the existing validated asset pipeline.

Common working conventions include:

- Logical world grid of 32×32.
- Environment source tiles commonly 64×64.
- Transparent object sprites.
- Opaque ground tiles where required.
- Bottom-centre ground anchors.
- Dedicated larger architecture sprites.
- Consistent character proportions.
- Appropriate 64px and 128px collectible plates.
- Nearest-neighbour world rendering.

These are technical conventions, not restrictions that override believable physical scale.

Document exceptions clearly.

## 49. Technical tiles are not illustrations

This distinction is mandatory.

Ground tiles, transitions, wall courses, roof connections and repeatable architecture require exact geometry.

They cannot be treated as isolated illustrated icons.

Validate:

- Exact dimensions.
- Opacity.
- Required transparency.
- Matching edges.
- Directional connections.
- Repeatability.
- Corner compatibility.
- Anchors.
- Collision metadata.
- Adjacent material compatibility.

Where exact matching is required, build or correct assets programmatically using existing canonical textures.

Never assume an AI-generated tile is seamless merely because the prompt requested seamless artwork.

## 50. Validation is not optional

Runtime packs must contain technically validated assets only.

Separate:

- Concept artwork.
- Source artwork.
- Pose references.
- Technical candidates.
- File-validated runtime assets.
- In-game validated assets.
- Creator-approved assets.

Never present these statuses as interchangeable.

An asset with the correct PNG dimensions is not necessarily artistically correct.

A visually attractive building is not necessarily compatible with the existing collision or perspective system.

A successful edge check does not guarantee attractive placement.

Do not mark assets approved without the required review.

## 51. Character consistency

Preserve approved characters.

Do not regenerate an existing inhabitant from a loose textual description.

New animation frames and portraits must preserve:

- Facial identity where established.
- Clothing.
- Accessories.
- Proportions.
- Material colours.
- Silhouette.
- Orientation.
- Visual scale.

Where identity cannot be preserved reliably, retain the existing asset and document the missing requirement.

## 52. Asset delivery workflow

Use the established GitHub staging branch:

https://github.com/severgrow/zaisaku/tree/art/mineral-analog-packs/art-packs

Maintain:

- Numbered immutable asset packs.
- Separate runtime and source archives.
- Maximum 10 MB per ZIP.
- Asset manifests.
- Validation reports.
- Checksums.
- Integration instructions.
- Approval status.
- Superseded asset records.

Do not overwrite historical packs.

Do not include unnecessary source artwork in production bundles.

Do not claim an archive has been uploaded without verifying its existence on GitHub.

## 53. Reuse before regeneration

Before requesting a new asset, ask whether an existing asset can solve the requirement through:

- Correct placement.
- Appropriate scaling.
- Layer separation.
- Palette variation.
- Combination with another asset.
- Shared procedural effects.
- Better composition.

Do not generate new artwork merely because an existing scene looks unfinished.

Often the problem is art direction, scale, lighting or placement rather than insufficient assets.

# PART XII — PERFORMANCE AS AESTHETICS

## 54. A beautiful game must feel responsive

Performance is part of the artistic identity.

A beautifully illustrated world that stutters, flashes or responds slowly is not a premium experience.

Protect:

- Fast startup.
- Stable frame pacing.
- Responsive input.
- Efficient asset loading.
- Appropriate texture filtering.
- Reliable camera movement.
- Smooth transitions.
- Consistent memory usage.

Avoid expensive visual effects that provide little artistic benefit.

Prefer lightweight procedural systems and cached textures.

## 55. Pixel rendering

Maintain crisp, consistent world rendering.

Use nearest-neighbour texture filtering where appropriate.

Avoid:

- Blurred sprites.
- Inconsistent pixel density.
- Shimmering edges.
- Unstable sprite positions.
- Unnecessary texture resampling.
- Visible seams caused by fractional placement.

Modern text and SVG interface elements should remain sharp independently of world rendering.

## 56. Atmosphere must scale

Lighting, shadows, particles and ambient animation should be implemented through reusable systems.

Future locations should inherit the same visual behaviour without requiring custom code for every object.

Use sensible effect budgets.

Respect reduced-motion settings.

Avoid independent animation loops for every lamp, plant or prop.

Build an atmosphere system that remains practical as the world grows.

---

# PART XIII — WHAT ZAISAKU MUST NEVER BECOME

## 57. Permanent exclusions

Avoid allowing the project to drift towards:

- Generic AI-generated pixel art.
- Medieval fantasy RPG aesthetics.
- Excessively Japanese visual motifs.
- Conventional steampunk.
- Neon-heavy cyberpunk.
- Cartoon mobile-game interfaces.
- Casino-like trading presentation.
- Anime character stereotypes.
- Decorative gold borders.
- Photorealistic textures.
- Inconsistent perspectives.
- Randomly generated architecture.
- Sticker-like building facades.
- Excessive glowing objects.
- Cluttered screens.
- Uniformly decorated environments.
- Unnecessary graphical complexity.
- Visually disconnected gameplay modes.
- Repeated generic asset generation without a defined need.

These exclusions are not limitations on creativity.

They protect the distinctiveness of ZAISAKU.

## 58. Do not confuse quantity with quality

More objects do not automatically create a better world.

More decorations do not automatically create atmosphere.

More lighting does not automatically create depth.

More animation does not automatically create life.

More interface elements do not automatically create usability.

A small district with exceptional composition and coherent visual behaviour is preferable to an enormous district filled with inconsistent artwork.

---

# PART XIV — THE ZAISAKU QUALITY GATE

## 59. Questions every agent must ask

Before approving a visual change, assess:

1. Does this look unmistakably like ZAISAKU?
2. Does it belong to the same civilisation as the existing artwork?
3. Does it communicate something about Zuisaku?
4. Is its perspective physically consistent?
5. Is its scale believable?
6. Does it possess a recognisable silhouette?
7. Does it feel modern rather than generically retro?
8. Does it have understated coolness?
9. Is it interesting without excessive decoration?
10. Does it preserve the established material language?
11. Is it readable on mobile?
12. Does it contribute to visual depth?
13. Does its lighting make physical sense?
14. Can it be expanded consistently through AI?
15. Can existing artwork be reused instead?
16. Does it work at actual gameplay scale?
17. Has its technical compatibility been verified?
18. Does it improve the experience rather than merely increase content?
19. Would removing something make the result stronger?
20. Would someone want to screenshot, explore, collect or remember it?

If the answer is repeatedly no, reconsider the design.

## 60. Visual review is mandatory

Do not approve a visual implementation simply because the code compiles.

Inspect actual screenshots and gameplay recordings.

Review desktop and mobile separately.

Compare against canonical approved references.

Look for scale, composition, atmosphere, alignment, lighting, perspective and artistic consistency.

Iterate based on what is actually visible.

Clearly distinguish generated mockups from implemented gameplay captures.

## 61. Creative autonomy

Development agents should make sensible, reversible artistic decisions within this constitution.

Do not ask the creator to approve every minor placement, spacing adjustment or shadow parameter.

However, do not independently change the fundamental visual identity.

Proposals that materially alter the palette, perspective, character designs, architecture, interface philosophy or cultural identity require explicit creator approval.

Be creative within the visual system.

Do not destroy the visual system in the name of creativity.

# PART XV — THE PERMANENT CREATIVE PRINCIPLE

## 62. The essence of ZAISAKU

ZAISAKU should feel like an unfamiliar civilisation experienced through a beautifully designed instrument.

Its world is material, biological, atmospheric and quietly mysterious.

Its technology is tactile, analog and strange.

Its inhabitants feel present.

Its architecture feels constructed.

Its objects feel desirable.

Its economy gives those objects meaning.

Its interfaces feel modern, precise and understated.

Its movement feels effortless.

Its visual identity should communicate depth without requiring excessive graphical complexity.

The game should be beautiful enough to appreciate, strange enough to explore, stylish enough to remember and simple enough to expand for years.

It should never feel like a collection of independently generated AI graphics.

It should feel like one coherent artistic vision.

**A different planet. A different civilisation. A different relationship with matter.**

**Mineral Analog in the world. Sealed Exchange in competition. Quiet Instrument in interaction.**

**Simple to create. Distinctive to experience. Deep enough to expand indefinitely.**

That is ZAISAKU.

Protect it.
